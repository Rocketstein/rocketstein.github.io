Technical Pipeline

The tooling separates artist authoring from runtime evaluation: editor-authored profiles, flow maps, and presets are stored as reusable assets, then consumed by the storm actor to drive cloud-material parameters at runtime.

Engineering challenge

Continuous noise motion without reset pops

The sampling strategy evolved in three steps: advance the noise offset continuously, wrap it into a fixed range, then crossfade two staggered phases so each reset occurs while its contribution is zero.

  1. 01Baseline

    Continuous scrolling

    The initial approach advances the 3D noise-sampling offset with time. Motion stays smooth, but the coordinates grow indefinitely.

    LimitationOver long runtimes, large sampling coordinates lose floating-point precision, making an eventual reset necessary.

  2. 02First fix

    Single-phase wrapping

    Applying frac(time) wraps the phase into a fixed range, keeping the time-varying sampling offset bounded over long runtimes.

    New artifactAt each 1 → 0 wrap, the sampling position jumps across the 3D noise field, causing an abrupt density change—a visible pop.

  3. 03Final solution

    Dual-phase crossfade

    Two phases offset by 0.5 independently sample and decode the low- and high-frequency (LF/HF) noise, then crossfade the results with complementary triangular weights.

    ResolutionAt each wrap, the wrapping phase has zero weight while the other carries the motion, hiding the discontinuity through the transition.

Outcome

The dual-phase crossfade removes visible reset pops and preserves continuous cloud motion over long runtimes, at the cost of evaluating both LF and HF noise twice.
